### 2. The problem and why it persists

#### Purpose

Show the problem and its cause as one continuous scene. Important everyday things live in different places, while existing tools each handle only one fixed moment. The person is left to remember how everything fits together and carry the missing lead-up mentally.

#### Problem copy

**Heading**

> Scattered everywhere. Carried in your head.

**Description**

> Birthdays sit in your contacts. Bills in your banking app. Maintenance relies on memory. Renewals get buried in your inbox. Remembering becomes another thing to manage, adding mental load until something slips or a last-minute reminder takes over.

#### Cause copy

**Heading**

> Dates have a place. The lead-up doesn’t.

**Description**

> Most tools are built around a fixed moment: due, alert, or do. What’s missing is the time beforehand: time to notice, think, and prepare.

**Tool roles**

- Calendar: Holds the date.
- Reminder: Alerts at one moment.
- To-do list: Holds what needs doing now.
- Missing: The time to prepare.

#### Illustration

- Show one person mentally occupied by everything they are trying to remember, surrounded by disconnected Calendar, Reminder, and To-do interfaces.
- Keep the human empathetic and mildly overwhelmed, not distressed.
- Use live HTML for the application roles and missing lead-up; use generated artwork only for the human figure.
- Make “Missing: The time to prepare” a literal break in the visual system, not another tool card, and use it to lead directly into the HeadsUp solution section.
- Keep the illustration open rather than enclosing it in a card. Place the full narrative on the left and the illustration on the right on desktop; stack the illustration below the narrative on mobile.
- Group Calendar, Reminder, and To-do as one row of equivalent problems above the person on desktop. Leave breathing room below the person, then state “Missing: The time to prepare” with stronger emphasis.

### 5. Cadence playground

#### Purpose

Let visitors understand when a task enters the HeadsUp Home page, how much preparation time remains, and what each cadence schedules after completion.

#### Refined copy

**Heading**

> Playground: Understand how HeadsUp keeps time.

**Description**

> Choose when a task is due, decide how early you want to see it, and explore what each cadence schedules next.

#### Playground controls

**When do you want a HeadsUp?**

- Task name
- Due date
- Preview date
- Give me a HeadsUp {amount} {period} before.

**What happens after completion?**

- If you complete it on
- Cadence type
- Recurrence settings

**Cadence type**

- One time
- Fixed schedule
- On completion

**Repeating-cadence inputs**

- Repeat every
- Time period
- Days, weeks, months, or years

**Fixed-schedule rules**

- Simple interval: Schedule the next due date a set number of days, weeks, months, or years after the current due date.
- Advanced rule: Schedule by a weekly, monthly, or yearly calendar pattern.

**Advanced weekly rule**

- Choose one or more weekdays.

**Advanced monthly rule**

- Add one or more occurrence and weekday combinations.

**Advanced yearly rule**

- Add one or more month, occurrence, and weekday combinations.

#### Live explanation

Present three connected statements:

**Your HeadsUp**

> {Task} appears on Home from {HeadsUp start date}, {HeadsUp window} before its due date.

**On the preview date**

> {Task status}. {Time until HeadsUp, time to prepare, overdue duration, or completion status}.

**What happens next**

> {Next due date and the rule it follows}.

#### One time

**Plain-language meaning**

> Happens once.

**Explanation**

> This task ends after completion. No next due date is scheduled automatically.

**Dynamic result**

> No next due date is scheduled automatically. You can choose a new date later if you need it again.

#### Fixed schedule

**Plain-language meaning**

> Follows the calendar.

**Explanation**

> The calendar keeps the next occurrence on schedule. Completion does not reanchor it.

**Dynamic result**

> The next due date is {next due date}, following {fixed schedule rule}.

#### On completion

**Plain-language meaning**

> Follows when you finish.

**Explanation**

> Completion sets the next due date.

**Dynamic result**

> The next due date is {next due date}, {interval} after completion.

#### Working illustration

Show the current and next occurrence as two lanes on one chronological timeline:

1. Current HeadsUp window begins
2. Current due date
3. Preview date
4. Completed on
5. Next HeadsUp window begins
6. Next due date

Long quiet periods may be compressed but must be labelled with their actual duration. If the current and next HeadsUp windows overlap, show and explain the overlap.

Changing the controls updates every affected date immediately:

- One time ends with no automatic next date.
- Fixed schedule remains anchored to the calendar and advances to the first matching date after completion if scheduled dates have already passed.
- A fixed schedule with multiple advanced patterns uses the earliest next matching date.
- On completion places the follow-up one selected interval after the actual completion date.
- The selected HeadsUp window is applied to both the current and next due date.

#### Behavioural source of truth

- The cadence types and labels come from the HeadsUp add-cadence flow: One Time, Fixed Schedule, and On Completion.
- One-time cadences close when completed, with an optional user-chosen next date.
- Fixed-schedule cadences advance from their calendar schedule, regardless of when they are completed.
- On-completion cadences advance from the actual completion date.
- Day and week intervals use calendar-day arithmetic. Month and year intervals preserve the intended day where possible and clamp safely for shorter months.
- A HeadsUp window controls when a cadence becomes eligible for the Home page. It does not change the cadence or due date.

#### Playground scope

- Run all calculations locally in the browser; the playground stores and submits nothing.
- Use labelled controls, visible validation, and a debounced live summary. Do not rely on timeline color alone.
- Allow past or future due dates and early or late completion so every meaningful state can be explored.
- Provide a Reset example action that restores the annual health-check scenario.

#### Product alignment note

The playground treats HeadsUp months and years as calendar periods. The HeadsUp application should align Home eligibility with the same rule so its form preview and Home behavior remain consistent.

### Founder stories

**Paul**

> The dates and numbers I could never hold on to.

> All my life, I have been terrible at remembering dates and numbers. I have forgotten to plan for my parents’ anniversary, let a sibling’s birthday slip my mind, missed card payments, overlooked meetings I needed to schedule, and forgotten client follow-ups. These lapses have cost me more than money. They have affected my relationships and work, disturbed my peace of mind, and chipped away at my confidence.

> I needed to help myself. That is why I came up with the idea for HeadsUp: a focused place to track not everything and anything, but the things that truly matter to me.

**Bhatt**

> The call I missed when it mattered most.

> I once promised to call someone close before a difficult appointment. Work filled my day, the promise slipped out of view, and I remembered the next morning. Missing that call did more than disappoint them. It left someone I cared about feeling alone when they had expected my support.

> My apology was sincere, but it could not recreate the moment. That experience made the problem feel human to me: remembering is often less about completing a task and more about showing up for another person. I designed HeadsUp to be a focused place for the commitments that matter, so they do not disappear inside a busy day.
